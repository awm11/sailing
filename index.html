    A top-down sailing dinghy driven by real sailing physics. Steer with the rudder, set the angle of the sail, and read the wind from the water around you.

    ← → to change

    The wind

    Wave crests lie across the wind and roll downwind. Darker patches are gusts. Moored boats always point into the wind.

    The sail

    The sail works like a wing. Pull it in until it stops flapping, but not so far that the air stalls. The trim chip tells you which.

    Balance

    The wind pushes the boat over sideways. Lean your weight out on the high side to keep it flat. In a strong gust, let the sail out as well, or you'll capsize.

    The no-go zone

    No boat can sail straight into the wind. To go upwind you zig-zag at about 45° to it, turning the bow through the wind each time (a tack).

    Right of way

    A boat with the wind on its left (port tack) must keep clear of one with the wind on its right (starboard tack). On the same tack, the boat further upwind keeps clear. Hitting a boat you should have avoided costs 10 seconds.
